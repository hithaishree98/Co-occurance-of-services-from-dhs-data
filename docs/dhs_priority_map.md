# DHS Priority Map — grounding reference for interpreting findings

STATUS: PROVISIONAL. Entries are from real DHS pages, but the mapping from our 22 service categories to DHS's site topic tags is drafted, not confirmed. Some services may have no clean corresponding tag. Validate the crosswalk at the grounding stage, per finding.

**How to use:** After the data produces an *observation*, look it up here. If the population/topic appears, the finding is "aligned with a published DHS priority" (Option 1 grounding). If it doesn't appear, the finding is either a *question to raise* (Option 2) or something to hold loosely. Source column is the evidence — always citable.

## Two things this table immediately teaches

1. **Coverage is uneven.** Homelessness, behavioral health, and opioids are *heavily* covered (many recent dashboards) — findings touching these are easy to ground. Older-adult services and family-strengthening have *little* recent public coverage — a finding there is a *gap*, which is itself worth noting ("this overlap doesn't correspond to a current public dashboard, suggesting an under-examined area").

2. **The overlaps DHS already publishes tell you which co-occurrences they consider legitimate.** ODS has a cross-system tab; the warehouse's own BH/Child-Welfare dashboard shows service overlap; QuickCount shows two-program overlap. So cross-system co-occurrence as a *concept* is pre-validated by their own tooling — you're not inventing the frame.
